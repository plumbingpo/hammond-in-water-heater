Tankless units are sophisticated machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Hammond, IN homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Error codes are a starting point, not a verdict, and we treat them that way on every Hammond call. The same ignition failure code can come from a worn igniter, a fouled flame rod, a gas line too small to feed the burner at full fire, or an air intake clogged with lint. That is why Hammond Water Heater tests before replacing anything. Our technicians read the fault history stored in the control board, watch flow sensor output with a faucet open, measure flame signal and igniter resistance, and put a manometer on the gas inlet to see what pressure actually reaches the valve when the burner is running flat out. We also compare inlet and outlet temperatures against the flow rate, which reveals a scaled heat exchanger long before it fails outright.

Once the cause is confirmed, most repairs are finished in a single visit. We descale heat exchangers with a pump and descaling solution circulated through the service valves, replace worn flow sensors, igniters, flame rods, thermistors, and combustion fans, clean inlet filter screens, and correct venting problems that trip pressure switches. When the real issue is a gas line sized for the old tank heater, we tell you plainly and quote the correct pipe run, because no amount of part swapping will fix a unit that is starved for fuel. If you are fighting a cold-water sandwich, we explain whether it is normal behavior for your model or a sign of a setting, fixture, or recirculation problem we can correct. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A fault code on the front panel and no hot water
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

From the outside a tankless heater looks like a plain box; inside, it is a tightly controlled system. One fault code often has several possible causes, and the sensors, igniters, and control boards involved are not cheap to buy on a hunch. Gas models add real hazards: the burner can fire at close to 200,000 BTU per hour, the vent carries combustion gases that contain carbon monoxide, and a loose gas connection or a vent joint that separates can put fuel or exhaust into the living space. Electric models bring a different hazard, often drawing well over 100 amps across several 240-volt circuits, so a loose lug or undersized wire can overheat quickly.

Calling a pro often costs less than trial and error. Our technicians do not guess at error codes. They test flow sensors under real flow, measure flame rod signal, confirm igniter resistance, read inlet gas pressure at full fire, and check the vent from end to end. When scale is the problem, we flush it out properly and rinse the system before the unit goes back in service. And because some tankless problems, like an undersized gas line or a cracked heat exchanger, are not solved by parts at all, we tell you so directly, before you commit to anything.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Check the inlet filter screen periodically, since a clogged one starves the unit of flow
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
