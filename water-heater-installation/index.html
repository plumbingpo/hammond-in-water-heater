How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Hot water running out before everyone has showered
  • A new jetted tub that outpaces the heater's capacity
  • A property where the water heater has to go in from scratch
  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • A planned switch from gas to electric, or to a heat pump model
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • An older install without an expansion tank, pan, or straps where required
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Installing a water heater from scratch pulls in gas, venting, electrical, and plumbing work all at once. Gas lines need sizing for the whole system's load, plus a leak test. Vents must meet the manufacturer's limits on length, diameter, slope, and clearances, because a bad vent can put carbon monoxide into living space. Electric, tankless, and heat pump heaters need circuits matched to their load, which can exceed what an older panel offers. On the plumbing side, the relief valve needs the right rating and a full-size discharge line, and depending on location, code may require an expansion tank, a drain pan, seismic straps, or a permit and inspection.

What you get from a pro is an installation that is done once. Our technicians plan the gas line, vent, and circuit before the first cut, take care of the permit when your jurisdiction requires one, and install the relief valve discharge and code items correctly. We test every joint, check every gas install for draft and carbon monoxide, and set the temperature low enough to prevent scalding. Rushed installs tend to leave undersized gas lines, bad vent pitch, and missing expansion tanks behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. And if the simpler option is the right one for your home, we say so plainly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Keep flammable liquids and clutter away from gas heaters
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Leave working space around the heater, and never stack storage against it
  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years to protect the tank
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
