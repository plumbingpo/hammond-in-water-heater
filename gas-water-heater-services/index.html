A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. When something goes wrong, it is usually at one of those points. In Hammond, IN, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Hammond technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • Leaks at the drain valve, the fittings, or the bottom of the tank
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A pilot light that will not stay lit
  • Any smell of gas around the appliance, which calls for leaving and phoning the gas utility
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Less hot water than the tank should deliver

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and working through them by guesswork is slow and costly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here's where to begin:

  • Have the anode rod inspected every few years and replaced when it is worn
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
  • Flush the tank on a regular schedule so sediment never bakes onto the bottom
  • Make sure the vent pipe stays connected, sloped upward, and free of rust
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Keep the area around the heater clear, especially of gasoline, paint, and solvents
