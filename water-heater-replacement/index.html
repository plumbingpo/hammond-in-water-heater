How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Banging and popping from a tank packed with sediment
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. Electrical work means matching breaker and wire gauge to the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

What you get from a pro is a replacement that is done once. The new heater is matched to your demand, the gas supply, venting, and panel are confirmed, and the permit is handled if your jurisdiction requires one. We route the relief valve discharge correctly, add the code items that apply, test for leaks and proper draft, and take the old tank with us. Rushed swaps tend to leave missing expansion tanks, bad vent pitch, and undersized gas lines behind. Ours are built to pass inspection and to last. And if a simple repair is the better call for your heater, we say so, without pressure.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Keep the space around a heat pump model clear so it can draw heat from the room
  • Drain a few gallons every six months so sediment never gets a foothold
  • Plan ahead: knowing the heater's age lets you replace it on your schedule, not after a leak
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Set the thermostat around 120 degrees to prevent scalding and reduce wear
